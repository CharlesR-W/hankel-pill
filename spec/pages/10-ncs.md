## 1. Reader outcome

3. Read the existing results on ResNet-20/CIFAR-10, DINOv2 ViT-S/14, Mamba-130m and Pythia-160m, with their caveats (kernel dependence, no privileged r, structure already present at initialization, retention is not memory).

5. 6. Say what the budget does not license: calling the sets computational states (closure is untested), linking them to logit rank (page 9), or using them for weights or out-of-distribution detection (open).

### 3.1 `ncs-cells` "Networks appear to treat whole regions of inputs alike" (≈ 420 words)

Placement: the hero sits between the lede and this heading (it belongs to 3.2's argument but is the page's picture). `ncs-fig-bowtie` is not here (3.2).

- Janiak et al. (2024) perturb and interpolate residual-stream activations of language models and find **stable regions**: regions in which the model's output barely changes, separated by comparatively sensitive boundaries. The regions sharpen over training and with model size, appear to follow semantic distinctions, and are much larger than the linear regions (polytopes) of piecewise-linear network theory: many gates can change sign inside one stable region. Heimersheim & Mendel (2024) report the related **activation plateaus** and sensitive directions in GPT-2; Shinkle & Heimersheim (2025) find that plateaus emerge gradually across layers, come from the MLP blocks, and have boundaries where the MLPs' input-output Jacobian norms spike. The programme calls these regions **activation cells** (glossary "cell": not a polytope, not a Voronoi cell of a discretization).
- If such cells exist, which cell an input lands in carries most of what the rest of the network uses, and the network behaves at that layer like a machine with discrete states (the programme's motivation, `CoherentModes/explore/cifar_coherent/site/content/overview.html:35-45`). Finding a cell directly means finding its boundary: Jacobians or interpolations in many directions around every input, at every layer, from data that sample the space sparsely. That does not scale.
- Reframe the question about groups instead of boundaries: **which inputs does the rest of the network treat as interchangeable?** A relaxation makes this computable (edge E20, schematic; section 8 hook): suppose the downstream output is nearly constant within cells, and the neighbourhood graph on inputs at a layer rarely links inputs in different cells. Then any downstream function g is nearly **harmonic** on the graph (it equals the average of its neighbours, Pg ≈ g, as in harmonic label propagation; Zhu, Ghahramani & Lafferty 2003), its graph energy comes only from links that cross a boundary, and cells are sets that few links leave. Finding such sets without labels or Jacobians is what spectral clustering does (page 4).
- The limit is a theorem: if **no** link crosses a cell boundary and each cell's graph is connected, the walk's eigenvalue 1 has multiplicity equal to the number of cells and its eigenspace is exactly spanned by the cell indicators (standard; von Luxburg 2007, Prop. 2 and §3); SEBA started from an aligned rotation returns the indicators exactly (SPARSITY S.7). What is not known is whether neighbourhood graphs of real activations respect plateau boundaries: the programme's labelled test of that hypothesis has been designed but not run (`overview.html:62-64`). Say so in the main text.

Key callout: "Stable regions motivate looking for groups of inputs that the network treats alike. Turning regions into low-cut sets is a relaxation; it is exact only when no neighbourhood link crosses a region's boundary."

Disclosures:
- "Why cluster inputs, not neurons or boundaries" (≤ 450 words): (1) **scalability**: the network enters only through one forward pass per probe input (and per checkpoint); after that, every operator is N × N on the inputs, independent of width; forward extraction still scales with the model, and dense operators cost O(N²d) to form and O(N²) to store (THEOREMS A.51); (2) **behavioural specificity**: the probe set is chosen, so the analysis can be restricted to inputs exercising one behaviour, and the resolution (r, bandwidth, block of layers) is explicit; (3) **a shared space**: the same N inputs index every layer whatever its width, so layers compose and compare without fitting (`THEORY.md` §1-§4.1; B.41: the operator determines the normalized Gram matrix); (4) **functions before partitions**: eigenfunctions superpose, so an input can take part in several modes; sets are a readout, not the primitive (`THEORY.md` §4.2). Point 2 is a design argument, not a result.
- "Expansion coding: why look in activation space at all" (≤ 350 words; edge E22, schematic, section 8 hook): reservoir computing and expansion coding (programme's "Slouching" post, part III.a) picture computation as many fixed random-ish features of the input with a learned linear readout; if that picture holds even partly, what the next stage can use is which inputs land near each other in activation space, which is the object this page decomposes. Neurons that read coherent sets of their inputs (page 7, Pughe-Sanford et al. 2025) are a second motivation. Neither derives the method; state that the post itself says the two threads are not yet connected.
- "The relaxation, stated precisely" (≤ 500 words): definitions (cell partition; downstream function g; walk P on inputs; energy $\frac12\sum_{ij}P_{ij}\|g_i - g_j\|^2$); the boundary-only energy statement; the isolated limit with its hypotheses (no crossing links, each cell connected) and the S.7 recovery statement with its initialization caveat (S.7(4): arbitrary starts can fail); what would be needed to go from "nearly isolated" to a bound (a gap and Davis-Kahan, 3.4). Mark the link to plateaus as the motivating hypothesis it is.
- "In other fields: cell, plateau" (≤ 150 words): glossary entries; "plateau" here is not a loss plateau; "cell" is not a polytope.

### 3.2 `ncs-flow` "Following inputs through layers turns that into an SVD" (≈ 450 words; the product sentences moved to a disclosure to make room for the hero and ResNet sentences)

Placement: `ncs-fig-bowtie` after the third bullet. "Arriving here first?" aside (≤ 80 words) under the heading: a coherent set is a set of points that stays together under a dynamics; it is found from the leading singular vectors of a transfer operator, and spectral clustering finds low-cut sets from a graph's leading eigenvectors (pages 3, 4); SEBA turns eigenvectors into sparse set indicators (page 5).

- **Sites and flow.** Fix N inputs. At every layer each input has an activation; the inputs are the same at every layer, so following them through depth is a flow of data points. Depth plays the role of time (edge E19, schematic for real networks; section 8 hook): a residual layer $x \mapsto x + f(x)$ resembles one step of a flow, though a general residual map need not be injective while a flow map is, and no bound ties real layers to a flow. The running example's face C is exactly such a flow by construction: twelve residual blocks each moving points along the gradient flow of the four-well potential for time T/12 (SPEC 3.5).
- **One walk per layer.** At layer ℓ, compare inputs by their (centred, normalized) activations, turn the similarities into a kernel, and balance it with Sinkhorn scaling into a symmetric, nonnegative, doubly stochastic matrix $P^{(\ell)}$: a random walk on the inputs whose steps stay among inputs that layer treats as similar (THEOREMS A.1-A.2). Balancing gives every layer the same uniform stationary weight, so walks of different layers can be combined without spurious loss of the constant mode (A.3-A.4).
- **Across a block of layers.** Average the walks, $\bar M = \frac1m\sum_\ell M^{(\ell)}$ with the fixed-lazy $M = (I + P)/2$ (the construction run on every real network here). A function on inputs that is slow for $\bar M$ changes little along the links of every layer at once; $I - \bar M$ is a finite-graph analogue of Froyland's dynamic Laplacian (edge E18, page 4). One sentence: the register's default composes the walks in order instead, which needs singular values and left/right subspaces (disclosure "Products of layers").
- **Read the sets.** Take the leading r eigenfunctions (constant included); SEBA rotates and soft-thresholds them into r sparse membership vectors; each input goes to its largest positive membership or stays unassigned. Sets are groups of **inputs**, never neurons or weights.
- Show the hero's three colourings, each a different question about the same tracked inputs: layer 0's sets (which inputs **start** close: a uniform cloud, cut along the box's shape), layer 12's sets (which inputs **end** close: four tight clumps, which on this toy are the basins, because the flow ends in wells), and the block average's sets (which inputs **stay** close: a set is cheap for $\bar M$ when its boundary is cheap at every layer on average, since its conductance under $\bar M$ is the mean of its per-layer conductances). Because the inputs are tracked, every labelling can be drawn at every layer, the last layer's included; the page must not say that only the average shows where inputs end up. The hero's strip shows what each labelling costs at each layer; quote the builder's comparison (`p10.json` `hero.compare`) as computed, whichever way it comes out. One sentence on the toy's limit: its flow ends in wells, so the last layer already sorts inputs by basin; a network whose last layer has no such wells is where the two questions come apart.
- One sentence on a real network (programme's ResNet-20 study, final checkpoint; `real.json` keys `f5.b1_vs_b1-logits.final.*`): layer b1's own leading mode has Rayleigh retention $f^\top Mf/\|f\|^2$ .94 on b1's walk but .73 on the pool's walk, while the block b1 → logits mode has .83 and .96 (mean .91); these are Rayleigh retentions of signed eigenfunctions, not fractions of mass; the block picks the split that layer b6 draws (|cos| .96), not b1's (.70). Details and the source's "half of this holds by construction" caveat are in the disclosure.
- The bowtie for this page: left, functions of inputs at the start of the block; right, at its end; knot, the block's leading eigenvalues; both sides weighted uniformly (Sinkhorn).

Key callout: "NCS groups inputs, not neurons. One layer's operator asks which inputs are close there; a block operator asks which stay close across the block, and its sets are read from the leading eigenfunctions of the averaged walk."

Disclosures:
- "Two constructions: the one analysed and the one run" (≤ 700 words), including the coverage table of section 6.3 of this spec. Theory construction (`THEORY.md` §3-4; `THEOREMS.md`): dense sphere kernel $K_\beta[i,j] = \exp(\beta\langle\hat\phi_i,\hat\phi_j\rangle)$ on normalized views that are fixed functions of the input (on face C, centred by a stored quadrature estimate $m_\ell$ of the population mean, SPEC 3.5), diagonal kept, symmetric Sinkhorn P (PSD, spectrum in [0,1]), ordered product Q as default. Implemented construction (`explore/cifar_coherent/README.md:50-91`; same for ViT and Mamba, `explore/ncs_report/site/content/overview.html`): cosine distances between probe-centred, row-normalized activations; union of directed 15-nearest-neighbour graphs with weight 1 on edges and $10^{-6}$ on every other off-diagonal pair, diagonal 0; Sinkhorn to a zero-diagonal doubly stochastic P (not necessarily PSD; the scaling exists and is unique because the floor gives the kernel total support, Sinkhorn & Knopp 1967, Csima & Datta 1972, not by A.1, which needs strictly positive entries or a positive diagonal); fixed-lazy $M = (I+P)/2$ (spectrum in [0,1]); block **average** $\bar M$; ordered products of centred lazy walks offered as an alternative (`README.md:185-205`). Average and product agree closely on short blocks and less on long ones: at the final ResNet checkpoint, r = 4, average↔product matched Jaccard falls from .99 (2 sites) to .64 start / .77 end for the full stem→logits block (`README.md:198-201`). Close with: "Every guarantee quoted below names the construction it covers."
- "Average or product?" (≤ 300 words): `THEORY.md` §4.1: with $P^{(k)} = I - \epsilon\mathcal L_k + O(\epsilon^2)$ the product and the mean share the averaged generator only when the product's bandwidth per step is $\epsilon/m$; at finite bandwidth they differ. A planted case in which a split strong in all but one layer beats a moderate persistent split under the average and loses under the product (`test_product.py`, README). Static image: `avgprod.png` (copied, see 5.6).
- "On a real network: one layer against a block" (≤ 250 words), static image `static_dynamic.png` (ResNet-20, final step: ψ₁ of the single snapshot b1 vs the block b1→logits, painted on five sites, with per-site retention), caption adapted from its source caption (`explore/cifar_coherent/site/content/findings.html`, "One snapshot's split against a block's"), facts from `questions.json` `facts.f5.*` (retentions .94/.71/.73/.81 and .83/.96, λ₁ .91, best static site b6 at |cos| .96, |cos| with b1's own .70; the agreement with the start site's own ψ₁ rises as the block starts later, median .82), with the source's own reading that half of the effect holds by construction.
- "Products of layers: singular blocks and coherent pairs" (≤ 450 words; the theory construction's default, not in the live widget). An ordered product $Q = P^{(\ell_m)}\cdots P^{(\ell_1)}$ of symmetric walks is bistochastic (A.3) but generally not symmetric: its eigenvectors need not be orthogonal and its spectrum need not be real. The right objects are singular values and **left and right** singular subspaces: right singular functions live on the inputs as seen at the start of the block, left ones at its end (computed by Lanczos bidiagonalization with Q and $Q^\top$ as pairs of layer matvecs, never a dense product). The ledger entries change accordingly: perturbation, A.26 (product error ≤ sum of layer errors) and Wedin's bound A.22, $(1+\sqrt2)e/(\delta - e)$ for each side, δ the singular gap; sampling, A.55 for two layers, singular values within $E_Q = O(N^{-1/4})$ and lifted projectors within $8E_Q/\delta$ (with the scalar chain for singular values analogous to 3.4's); rounding, S.4's coherent pairs (a source set A and an image set B with transported mass fraction; a witness exists for $\sigma > 1/2$, the trimmed transport bound is positive only for $\sigma > 17/18$; register indexing shifted), not the symmetric sweep S.3; truncation, A.6 still exact on either side, but S.10's retained count does not transfer ($\sigma_i(Q^t) \ne \sigma_i(Q)^t$ in general). A.5 gives a near-one singular value its meaning (a two-stage low-dissipation set). The implemented construction offers ordered products of centred lazy walks as an alternative (`README.md:185-205`); on ResNet-20, average and product agree less on long blocks ("Two constructions" disclosure).
- "In other fields: coherent set, site, retention" (≤ 200 words): glossary; "site" here means an input (the reports sometimes use it for a layer location); retention is a Rayleigh quotient $f^\top Mf/\|f\|^2$, not memory.

### 3.4 `ncs-budget` "The budget lists guarantees and gaps; a run earns certificates" (≈ 450 words: the bullets below are the content, and the writer meets SPEC 5.2's cap by moving detail into the "Sampling" and "Chain F" disclosures, never by dropping the ledger/certificate distinction or the end-to-end equation)

Centrepiece: `ncs-fig-budget`. The section's prose is the map; the widget and the disclosures carry the formulas. All theorem numbers are CoherentModes `THEOREMS.md` (A.x, B.x) and `SPARSITY.md` (S.x), with the index shift stated once (side note).

- **Ledger and certificate** (open the section with this). The budget is a **ledger**: for each term, the strongest result the register has, its hypotheses and the construction it covers, or a named gap. A **certificate** is what one run earns when those hypotheses are checked on it and the bound evaluated; the widget gives every row a status: certified, vacuous, unavailable (a hypothesis fails) or no theorem. Say plainly where things stand: on the toy network every row is evaluated live; the real-network runs of 3.3 have no certificate yet.
- **Three objects on the same inputs, one off them.** On the N inputs actually used: the operator computed, $P^{\rm used}$ (chosen kernel and scale, possibly sparsified, possibly with noisy activations, one layer or averaged over the block), and a reference operator $P^{\rm ideal}_N$ (the same construction at its reference scale, dense, noise-free). Separately, the reference population operator $P_{\star,\rm ref}$: the population (infinite-sample) version of the reference construction, the noise-free fixed view at the reference bandwidth $\beta_{\rm ref}$, which acts on functions on the whole data distribution, not on $\mathbb R^N$; it is the only population target the page names. A resolution r, and the gap $\delta_r$ separating the top r eigenvalues (constant included) from the rest.
- **Chain F, finite sites (P, Z, layers).** Kernel, scale and activation perturbations (**P**), sparsification (**Z**) and averaging over layers all produce operator-norm errors between symmetric matrices on the same $\mathbb R^N$, so they add: $e_F = e_P + e_Z$ per layer, then the mean over the block. If $e_F < \delta_r/2$, the block moves by at most $e_F/(\delta_r - e_F)$ (A.21). This is a certificate for the computed block against the declared reference, for **either** construction, once $\|P^{\rm used} - P^{\rm ideal}_N\|$ is bounded above by a verified computation (5.4: Rump's floating-point test, or a rounding-inflated Frobenius norm; a Lanczos value is only a lower estimate); a priori constants exist only for the analysed construction (A.24-A.25, S.2; A.23 needs strictly positive kernels, which the implemented zero-diagonal kernel is not).
- **End to end, for spectral values** (displayed equation; the page's strongest end-to-end statement). For one sphere-kernel layer, with the reference at $\beta_{\rm ref}$ on the noise-free fixed view and the used operator at any bandwidth, noise level or sparsifier, with probability at least $1 - \eta_{\rm fail}$, simultaneously for every i and for every layer and sample size the widget offers,
  $$|\lambda_i(P^{\rm used}_N) - \lambda_i(P_{\star,\rm ref})| \le e_F + E_N(\beta_{\rm ref}),$$
  Weyl's inequality on the same inputs ($e_F \ge \|P^{\rm used}_N - P^{\rm ideal}_N\|_{op}$, whatever bandwidth the used operator has) plus A.44's eigenvalue comparison between the reference and its own population, both at $\beta_{\rm ref}$. Name the two terms (finite-site P and Z; sampling S) and the target: the declared reference population's eigenvalues and hence its gaps, not its eigenfunctions or sets, and not the population at the bandwidth in use. On the same event $\delta_r(P_{\star,\rm ref}) \ge \delta_r(P^{\rm ideal}_N) - 2E_N(\beta_{\rm ref})$, which is what a sampling certificate for the block needs.
- **Sampling (S), shown three ways.** A theorem certificate (A.44 for one walk, $E_N$ with the universal constant; A.55 for two-layer products at $N^{-1/4}$; A.56 counts documents, not positions), valid only for views fixed before the inputs are drawn, at a reference bandwidth chosen on an independent sample, with the failure probability split over every layer and sample size a reader can pick after looking; a plug-in calculation (the same formula with an estimated constant: not a certificate); and an empirical comparison with a larger reference sample (not population truth). For the layer **average** and for kNN graphs there is no sampling theorem (open; section 8 hook). Subspace certificates in different lifts cannot be summed without a common identification: chain F's lives on $\mathbb R^N$, the sampling one in a lift (SPEC 4.3), so only the scalar statement above runs end to end.
- **Rounding (R).** From a subspace to sets: for a candidate partition H, the residual $\eta = \|(I - VV^\top)H\|_F$; if S.8's conditions hold, there **exists** an aligned frame whose threshold step is within $2\sqrt2\eta/c$ of H. That is an existence statement, not a guarantee for the SEBA run shown (S.7), whose own error is reported beside it; a two-way sweep cut has conductance at most $\sqrt{2(1-\lambda_2)}$ (S.3).
- **Truncation (T).** Exact: the part of any activation matrix F outside the r retained modes has squared norm $\sum_{i>r}\|F^\top\psi_i\|^2$ (A.6). The retained count $\#\{i : \lambda_i^t > \vartheta\}$ is the smallest rank approximating the t-step walk to spectral error ϑ (S.10).
- **Interpretation (I).** No bound (3.6).
- Walk through the widget's first tour in three sentences: which rows are certified, which vacuous, which unavailable or without a theorem, and why (quote the builder's recorded statuses, `p10.json` `tours.default.status`).

### 3.5 `ncs-knob` "The knob decides which resolutions can be certified" (≈ 420 words)

Centrepiece: `ncs-fig-knob`.

- Truncation error falls as r grows (A.6's tail can only shrink).
- Subspace certificates divide by $\delta_r$ (chain F's $e_F/(\delta_r - e_F)$, A.44's $4E_N/\delta_{\rm lb}$); eigenvalue and operator-error bounds (chain F's $e_F$, the end-to-end $e_F + E_N$) do not, but the gap decides whether those errors are small enough to distinguish the block (below $\delta_r/2$ they separate it); rounding additionally needs a small partition residual η and a positive threshold margin c (S.8). Gaps are **not monotone in r**. On the running example's dynamics (SPEC 3.2) short lags certify the four wells and long lags only the two pairs; on face C the bandwidth plays the lag's part, and the widget's panel (c) shows the builder's $\delta_2$ and $\delta_4$ against it (quote `p10.json` `faceC.scan`; do not predict the order).
- The ideal companion (tagged "ideal companion (B.33), not the running example"): two coarse groups × two fine clusters of identical inputs at fine and coarse prototype distances $d_f$, $d_c$. B.33 gives every eigenvalue in closed form: $\lambda_c = 1 - Gb_fz_c/Z$, $\lambda_f = (1 - z_f)/Z$, $z_f = e^{-\beta d_f}$, $z_c = e^{-\beta d_c}$, $Z = 1 + (b_f - 1)z_f + b_f(G-1)z_c$. As β grows, the fine gap keeps opening, while the coarse gap first opens, peaks at an intermediate bandwidth and then closes (at the defaults: 0.31 at β = 1, a maximum ≈ 0.575 near β ≈ 3.3, 0.17 at β = 12; quote the live values): **the bandwidth decides which resolution is well separated, and not monotonically**.
- The price: for a fixed target accuracy, a single walk needs $N \propto \delta_r^{-2}$ (A.44's condition $E_N \le \delta_r/4$ with $E_N \propto N^{-1/2}$) and an ordered product $N \propto \delta_r^{-4}$ (A.55); A.44's constant grows like $e^{3\beta}$ (population-sensitive) to $e^{6\beta}$ (worst case). Larger β resolves finer clusters and makes the sampling certificate exponentially more expensive. For the companion, $S_{\max}$ is known in closed form (6.5), so panel (b) shows both forms.
- On a real network: the ResNet block's gaps of .023 after four modes and .005 after five (3.3) mean that the finite-site chain would need $e_F$ below .0115 or .0025 to certify those resolutions. Empirically, recomputing the same blocks on 10,000 images instead of 2,000 changes the sets more at higher r, though not monotonically (panel (d), `d/bn/reps.json` `n10k`).

Key callout: "Finer resolution is cheaper to describe and dearer to certify. The bandwidth decides where the gaps open, and the data needed grow exponentially with it."

Disclosure "The maths: why gaps are not monotone, and the ideal companion" (≤ 500 words): S.10's gap function $g_r(t) = \lambda_r^t - \lambda_{r+1}^t$ rises then falls in diffusion time t (site indexing); B.33's derivation sketch (equal row sums; four invariant subspaces); its threshold windows (coarse: $Gb_fe^{-\beta d_c} < 1/2$ and $\beta d_f < 1/2$; fine: $e^{-\beta d_f} < 1/(Gb_f + 1)$), noting they are sufficient conditions for "exactly G (or Gb_f) eigenvalues above 1/2", not the gaps themselves; and why the coarse gap $\lambda_c - \lambda_f$ rises and then falls (both eigenvalues tend to 1 as β grows, $\lambda_c$ first), while the fine gap $\lambda_f$ only rises.

### 3.6 `ncs-not` "At best a certificate covers a readout, not a computational state" (≈ 330 words)

- **Interpretation has no bound.** Where its hypotheses are checked, a run's certificates say that a spectral block of a declared operator is stable against the named perturbations, how far its eigenvalues can be from the population's, and that an aligned sparse readout near a candidate partition exists. They do not say that the set is what the network computes with. Three reasons, each with its source: a construction asked for r sets will often return r sets (on structureless Gaussian data, the implemented construction returns about four effective sets at r = 4, `explore/cifar_baselines/NOTES_B.md:128`); structure can predate learning (3.3); and in idealized settings even a perfect belief-state layer can carry more, or infinitely many, modes than the hidden states it encodes (B.10-B.12).
- **Coarse-grainings, not states** (edge E21, schematic, hook). Calling a coherent set a computational state would need closure: that the coarse-grained description predicts its own future as well as the fine one does (page 6; Rosas et al. 2024). No closure test has been run on a network. The programme's own analogy to automaton states is flagged there as an analogy only: one fixed continuation, neighbourhoods rather than outputs, no state-count theorem (`overview.html:116-121`).
- **Outside and inside** (edge E17, schematic, hook). Page 9's logit table and this page's operators are two estimates of predictive structure. The outside view's budget has a truncation term and no certified sampling term; the inside view's ledger has all six rows, some of them gaps. A two-column table (static HTML, not a figure) lists the rows side by side. No theorem connects a model's logit rank to its coherent sets.
- **Open directions**, named in one sentence each, with the hook: a weight-space version (no construction yet); out-of-distribution detection (a new input's Nyström row, A.45, could be scored against the sets; the extension divides by eigenvalues and has not been tested as a detector).

Key callout: "Calling a coherent set a computational state would require closure: that the coarse description predicts its own future. Nobody has tested that for a network."

### 6.1 Face C and what is precomputed

- Face C exactly as SPEC 3.5: N inputs uniform on $[-1.4,2.6] \times [-1.0,1.4]$ (SPEC 3.5's input box), seed 0; 12 residual layers of the gradient flow (RK4, 8 substeps), T = 0.3; $D_{\rm flow}$ a control; κ = 0. **Lift on by default** in every widget on this page: $a^{(\ell)} = \tanh(W_{\rm lift}x^{(\ell)} + c_{\rm lift})$, $W_{\rm lift}$ 64 × 2 with entries $N(0, 1.5^2)$, $c_{\rm lift}$ ~ U(−1, 1), seed 7; then centring and row normalization: for `kNN`, the implemented preprocessing (subtract the mean over the probe inputs at that layer); for `sphere`, the stored quadrature estimate $m_\ell$ of the population mean (`faceC.popmean`, SPEC 3.5), so that each view is a fixed function of the input as A.40-A.44 require. Reason: the register's sampling theorems (A.40-A.44) and view/temperature corollaries (A.24-A.25) are stated for normalized views; on raw 2-D positions, normalization keeps only the angle, which is not the example's geometry. Raw positions remain an advanced option with Euclidean kernels and a stated loss of chain S. SPEC 3.5 makes this the default on this page.
- Ground truth: the basin (quadrant at κ = 0) of each input; uncommitted band as SPEC 3.5.
